I build software and teach people how to build it.

Ledgers, indexes, marketplaces, mobile apps and the instruments that test them. Then I teach the building of it, along with the design and the marketing around it.

A fee ledger that reconciles itself. A room of people writing their first working program. Both are the same job.

The work runs wider than one kind of product. A fee ledger that reconciles M-Pesa payments for a school of 217 students. A national index that ranks brands on a weighted model rather than an average of stars. A marketplace the size of a neighbourhood, a crop diagnosis app that works with the radio off, an Android instrument built to measure whether a product is worth making at all.

The other half of the week is a classroom, and it is not onboarding. I head training at the Siaya Community Digital Hub, where 1,576 people have come through, and the programmes teach people to build: web development, data analysis, cyber security, design and video. Children writing their first Scratch block, school leavers finishing with something they built themselves.

Teaching is not a side project. It is half the practice.

I write the curriculum, produce the material and stand in the room. The approach is the same every time: show what is happening underneath before handing anyone a shortcut, and use examples people already recognise. M-Pesa transactions and school fees teach a loop better than foo and bar ever will.

The subjects run wider than code. Digital marketing and social media management, Facebook especially, where most small businesses here are already trying to sell and mostly guessing. Design and production in Blender, Canva and DaVinci Resolve. And how to actually work with AI tools, Claude and Claude Code in particular, which is the fastest moving thing in this list and the one with the most confident nonsense written about it.

Beyond Form 4 is the main programme

It is for the people who have just sat their final school exams and are waiting, which in this part of the country can mean a year of waiting. Anyone past Form 4 can take it. Nobody needs to arrive knowing anything.

Everyone does the same five courses first: computer fundamentals, ICT and society, internet and web technologies, productivity tools, and digital marketing. That is the floor. Then each learner picks one of five specialisations and goes deep enough in it to be employable rather than merely familiar.

  • Web Development12 topics, 11 skills, around 40 hours
  • Graphic Design16 topics, 14 skills, around 40 hours
  • Data Analysis12 topics, 13 skills, around 40 hours
  • Cyber Security13 topics, 12 skills, around 40 hours
  • Videography13 topics, 11 skills, around 30 hours

The programme is run inside EduLedger, which is the other half of this site. The curriculum, the specialisation tagging, the attendance, the assessments and the certificates at the end are all the software further up this page. I teach on the thing I built, which is the fastest way I know to find out what is wrong with it.

I started in Turkana, fixing things that had to work without ideal conditions.

My first job in tech was as an ICT field officer in Lodwar. Turkana teaches you quickly that a system is only as good as the power, the connection and the person in front of it. I have built everything since with that in mind, which is why the work tends to include the parts nobody credits: the server in the corner, the DNS filtering, the tunnel that gets a machine past CGNAT.
