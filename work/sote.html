Sote

A marketplace the size of a neighbourhood, where not every exchange involves money.

Role
Design, development, deployment
Company
Bitroot
Year
2026
Status
Deployed, still building

The problem

The national classifieds sites are national, which is the whole trouble. A sofa listed forty kilometres away is not really for sale to you, and a stranger who wants to meet somewhere neither of you knows is a risk that most people correctly decline. So the actual trade happens in WhatsApp groups, where nothing is searchable, nothing is accountable and the same three items circulate until someone gives up.

The second thing those sites get wrong is assuming every exchange is a sale. A lot of what moves between neighbours is a swap, a loan, a gift, or food that will not survive the week. Force all of that through a listing form with a price field and most of it simply never gets listed.

Safety is not a feature to add later. It is the reason anyone shows up at all.

What it does

You sign in with your phone number, tell it roughly where you live, and see what is being offered near you. A listing is one of five kinds, and the form changes to match, so a barter listing asks what you want in return and a cooked meal listing asks when it expires rather than pretending either of them is a sale.

  • Five listing modes, trade, barter, gift, donate and meal, each validated on its own terms
  • Phone number sign in with a one time code, no password to forget
  • Reputation scored separately per mode, so a good trader is not automatically a good barterer
  • A full exchange lifecycle, proposed, accepted, confirmed, completed, rather than a chat that trails off
  • Reviews that can only be written after an exchange actually completed, one per person per exchange
  • Barter posting gated behind five completed deals, so the hardest mode to get right is not the first one a stranger tries

Reputation per mode

A single star rating flattens things that are not alike. Someone can be entirely reliable at selling a phone for an agreed price and quite unreliable at judging whether the bicycle they are offering is worth your generator, and those are different risks to the person on the other side.

So the score splits three ways, trading, bartering and giving, and each only moves when an exchange reaches completed. It cannot be inflated by posting, by signing up, or by a conversation that went nowhere. Barter is then gated behind five completed exchanges, which is a deliberate piece of friction: it is the mode where disputes come from honest disagreement about value rather than from bad faith, and it is not where a new account should begin.

How it was built

Two applications rather than one framework doing both. An Express API with Prisma over PostgreSQL owns the data, the exchange state machine and the reputation arithmetic, and a Next.js front end on the App Router talks to it over a plain REST surface. Images go to Cloudinary rather than onto the server disk. Both run under PM2 and reach the public through a Cloudflare Tunnel, sharing a box with EduLedger on their own ports.

Every page was laid out for a phone first and adapted upward, which is the opposite of how the earlier version was written and the reason it had to be rewritten. Almost nobody will open this on a desktop. The design leans on a serif for display text and colour coded modes, so the kind of a listing is legible before the text is read.

What is not built yet

It is deployed and it is not finished, and the gap is worth stating plainly. The radius filter is the clearest example. The interface says three kilometres and the query currently matches on the neighbourhood name rather than on coordinates, which is close enough to be useful and is not what it claims. Every field needed to do it properly already exists in the schema, so the work is the query and the permission prompt, not a migration.

Larger pieces are missing entirely. There is no messaging, no payment or escrow, no identity verification, and the safe meeting points exist as a table with nothing reading or writing it. Reviews and the exchange lifecycle are real and finished. I would rather this page name the difference than let a feature list imply a product that is further along than it is.

Where it goes

Real geography first, because it is the promise on the front of the product. Then meeting points, which is the feature that decides whether two strangers complete an exchange or quietly stop replying, and which is only worth building once there are enough listings for the question to come up.

The principle I am trying to hold to is one neighbourhood at a time. A marketplace that is thin everywhere is worth less than one that is dense in a single place, and every temptation so far has been to widen it before it was worth being in.
