The hub

The Siaya Community Digital Hub is where the teaching half of my practice happens. This page is the room itself: the programme, the developer community that formed around it, the partners who have brought work through the door, and the people who have walked in to see it.

The presidential launch

Siaya

The hub was launched by President William Ruto, with Senator Oburu Oginga and the Principal Secretary for ICT present. I spent part of that day explaining Beyond Form 4 to the chief executives of the ICT Authority, Konza Technopolis and EldoHub.

Explaining a training programme to the people who fund training programmes is a particular kind of test. You find out quickly whether what you have built is a curriculum or just a list of topics.

Beyond Form 4

The main programme

Teaching, and cohorts through the programme

Mazingira Day in 2025, and a cohort day out

Five common courses, then one of five specialisations: web development, graphic design, data analysis, cyber security or videography. It is for people who have just sat their final school exams and are waiting, which here can mean a year of waiting.

The full programme structure

Networking and cabling

Hub summit, March 2026

Hands on training in networking and cabling, run at the hub during the March 2026 summit

Structured cabling and network setup, taught on real equipment rather than on a diagram. This is the part of the work that decides whether everything else in a building functions, and it is almost never taught to the people who will actually have to maintain it.

Junior Tech Pioneers

Ages 7 to 17

Four levels each school holiday, ending in a demo day in front of guardians

Hub Lab, the developer community

Available on request

Hackathons, the October 2025 summit, and the people who turn up to them

Hub Lab is a community of developers I brought together. They assemble for hackathons and for builds larger than one person can carry, and they are available on my request.

Participants from the Siaya hackathon were invited on to the Eldoret summit afterwards, which is the part that actually matters. The measure of a community like this is not how many people attend once, it is how many get asked back by somebody else.

If a project needs more than one pair of hands, this is the answer, and a better one than a subcontractor nobody in the room has met.

How to work with the community

Partners and visitors

Who has come through the door

Ajira Digital for trainer training, the Kenya Film Commission running sessions with their own equipment inside the hub, and delegations from the Canadian and Russian embassies. A hub in Siaya being worth a diplomatic visit is not something I would have predicted, and it is the clearest signal that the work is being noticed well outside the county.
